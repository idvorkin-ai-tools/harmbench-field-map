LLM-optimizer attacks

One language model writes the prompts, another grades them — the family that made closed-model evaluation possible, at a fraction of GCG's compute.
TL;DR — Six methods that replace a gradient with a generator and a judge: propose prompts, query the target, score the reply, keep the best, mutate, repeat. The family's top entry, AutoDAN at 52.7, is 1.6 points off GCG's 54.3 with no backward pass — a gap smaller than the grader's own label noise. Four of the six can be pointed at an API, which is why the GPT, Claude and Gemini rows of Table 6 have numbers in them at all. Two caveats do heavy lifting: HarmBench swapped every closed-source attacker and judge for Mixtral 8x7B, and GPTFuzz ships in the repo with no Table 6 column — do not quote it an ASR.

PAP-top5 — zero target queries

The gradient family answers "what is the strongest attack if I own the weights". This family answers the more urgent question — what can I build with a chat endpoint and a budget — and the answer is "almost as much, for almost nothing".

The shape they share, and the access they actually need

Four of the six run the same loop. An attacker model proposes candidate prompts for one behavior; each goes to the target; a judge scores the reply; high scorers are kept, mutated or extended until something crosses a threshold or the budget runs out. What survives is one prompt per behavior, saved as the test case. Nothing differentiates through the target — the search signal is a scalar — so the target can in principle sit behind an HTTP call.

In principle. Read allowed_target_model_types in run_pipeline.yaml before assuming any of these is query-only. PAIR, TAP, TAP-Transfer and PAP-top5 carry [open_source, closed_source]. AutoDAN and GPTFuzz carry [open_source] only, and in both cases the code, not caution, is the reason: AutoDAN's fitness function is the target's own cross-entropy loss, a logits-level read rather than a chat turn, and GPTFuzz builds its target as a local vLLM engine with a RoBERTa judge on a spare CUDA device. Gradient-free is not the same claim as black-box, and this family contains both kinds — the distinction model_type in models.yaml silently enforces. All six take behavior_chunk_size: 5; five want base_num_gpus: 2 and GPTFuzz one, for the attack apparatus alone. Which raises the substitution underneath every number here:

"Some methods use closed-source LLMs in their original implementations, most often as an evaluator to guide optimization or as an attacker LLM to optimize test cases. We replace these with Mixtral 8x7B to reduce the costs of running our large-scale comparison. While this can reduce their effectiveness, it has two substantial benefits: (1) reducing evaluation costs, and (2) enabling compute comparisons."— HarmBench, §C.1

So each average is a lower bound on the published method and the ordering is the durable result — and every judge steering these searches is weaker than the Llama 2 13B classifier that grades the outcome.

Not shown here. AutoDAN and GPTFuzz seed from corpora of in-the-wild jailbreak templates that ship in the repository, and PAP carries forty persuasion templates with worked examples. This page gives their size, structure and role in the search and reproduces none of their content — a deliberate omission, not an oversight.

AutoDAN — Generating Stealthy Jailbreak Prompts on Aligned LLMs

needs target loss (open source only)class AutoDAN100 steps × 64 candidatesavg ASR 52.7

A genetic algorithm over whole prompt prefixes. The population is 64 strings, initialised from the first 64 entries of a 128-item corpus of handcrafted persona jailbreaks shipped as a ~415 KB Python list. Each generation all 64 are assembled as prefix + behavior in the target's chat template, run forward once, and scored by the target's cross-entropy loss on a fixed continuation; low loss wins. Then the genetics: num_elites: 0.1 — six of 64 — survive untouched, the other 58 slots come from softmax roulette selection over the negated losses, paired and recombined at crossover: 0.5 by splitting both parents on sentence boundaries and swapping at num_points: 5 cut points. Only mutation: 0.01 of offspring — about 58 across a whole run — go to mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 under vLLM for a paraphrase (a GPT mutator sits commented out). The "LLM optimizer" label is generous: AutoDAN is overwhelmingly sentence-level recombination of existing human jailbreaks, with a model called about one time in a hundred to stop the pool collapsing. The paper is blunter than the name:

"A semi-automated method that initializes test cases from handcrafted jailbreak prompts. These are then evolved using a hierarchical genetic algorithm to elicit specific behaviors from the target LLM."— HarmBench, §C.1

Every eval_steps: 5 generations (25 for Llama 2) it checks a 36-entry refusal-substring list holding bare tokens like never, matched anywhere — so early stopping under-fires and the emitted test case is the loss-argmin, not a verified success. The fields model_short_name and developer_name exist because the seed templates address the assistant by name. Result: 52.7 all behaviors, second column in the table — 68.3 standard, 68.4 contextual, 6.0 copyright — yet near-zero on the Llama 2 rows (0.5 / 0.8 / 2.8). Reach for it when you have weights and want GCG-class results without GCG's 500×512 search. Liu et al., 2023, arXiv:2310.04451 (verified).

PAIR — Jailbreaking Black Box Large Language Models in Twenty Queries

chat API onlyclass PAIR20 streams × 3 rounds ≤ 60 queriesavg ASR 40.7

The simplest honest feedback loop in the benchmark. An attacker model, told the behavior and the desired opening of the response, produces a candidate prompt plus a written self-critique of what it is changing; the candidate goes to the target; the reply comes back with a judge score attached; the attacker revises. HarmBench runs n_streams: 20 such conversations independently, steps: 3 deep — the PAIR paper's own defaults — putting the worst case at 60 target queries per behavior. Attacker and judge are both Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1 on two GPUs, with budgets attack_max_n_tokens: 500, target_max_n_tokens: 150, judge_max_n_tokens: 5 and cutoff_score: 10; the judge's 1–10 score is read by a bracket regex that falls back to 1 when unreadable.

Two details change how to read the number. The attacker only ever sees 150 tokens of the reply while step 3 grades 512 — the search is steered by a quarter of the grader's evidence. And for contextual behaviors PAIR queries the target without the ContextString during the loop, prepending it only to the final test case, where TAP passes it through on every query; PAIR's 60.5 contextual trails TAP's 61.8 and TAP-Transfer's 67.5, and the asymmetry is a plausible contributor. Overall 40.7 / 47.5 standard / 60.5 contextual / 7.5 copyright. Reach for it when 60 queries per behavior is your ceiling. Chao et al., 2023, arXiv:2310.08419 (verified).

TAP — Tree of Attacks: Jailbreaking Black-Box LLMs Automatically

chat API onlyclass TAPdepth 10 × branch 4, width 10avg ASR 45.2

PAIR with branching and a pre-filter. One root conversation (n_streams: 1) expands branching_factor: 4 ways per level for depth: 10 levels, frontier held to width: 10. The addition that matters is a second judge call: before any candidate reaches the target an on-topic judge is asked whether the rewrite still asks for what the behavior asked for, so branches where the attacker has drifted somewhere adjacent and harmless are pruned before they cost a query. A second prune, on harmfulness score, follows the target round.

Work the arithmetic on the shipped config, worst case: level 0 yields 4 candidates, level 1 yields 16, levels 2–9 yield 40 each — 340 attacker generations and 340 on-topic judge calls — but only 4 + 10 + 8×10 = 94 target queries, because the frontier is capped. Against PAIR's 60 target queries and 120 auxiliary calls, TAP buys its +4.5 points with roughly 1.6× the target traffic and six times the attacker-side compute: the better buy if your cost model is per-target-query, not if it is total FLOPs. One caveat on the pruning story — because phase 1 already truncates to width and both judges floor their score at 1 on an unparseable response, the phase-2 prune never removes a candidate in the default configuration.

45.2 all behaviors; 55.3 standard, 61.8 contextual, 8.5 copyright — the highest copyright cell of any of the sixteen columns, on a very low bar (see the copyright category). Reach for it when target queries are scarce and attacker compute is not. Mehrotra et al., 2023, arXiv:2312.02119 (verified).

TAP-Transfer — TAP run once against a surrogate, then replayed

no target access at replay timeclass TAP, experiment gpt-4-0613_judge_gpt-4-1106-preview_targetone attack run, 33 targetsavg ASR 48.3

Not a new algorithm — the same TAP class pinned to one experiment block, naming gpt-4-0613 as judge and gpt-4-1106-preview as target while the attacker stays Mixtral. TAP runs to completion against GPT-4 Turbo as a surrogate; the resulting test cases are replayed unmodified at every other model, one forward pass each.

It is also, on the closed rows, the best attack in the table: 54.8 on GPT-4-0613, 58.5 on GPT-4-Turbo-1106, 62.3 on GPT-3.5-Turbo-0613, against direct TAP's 43.0, 36.4 and 47.7. Replaying a prompt developed against a different model beats attacking that model directly, on exactly the models where adaptation should matter most — which says most of what TAP finds per behavior is a generic framing that works on chat-tuned models in general. The tree search is finding a good jailbreak, not a good jailbreak for you. The exceptions sharpen that: on Gemini Pro transfer loses (31.2 against 38.8), on all three Claude models it collapses to 0.8–1.5, below direct TAP and far below GCG-Transfer's 12.1 on Claude 1, and on Zephyr + R2D2 direct TAP wins 60.8 to 54.3.

One thing sits under this column: pinning a GPT judge routes it through the GPTJudge path, whose on_topic_score parses output with the numeric bracket regex — while the on-topic rubric asks for [[YES]] or [[NO]]. No digits, no match, so every candidate takes the fallback score of 1 and the phase-1 topicality prune degenerates into keeping width candidates by index. The open-source judge path uses the correct yes/no parser and is unaffected. TAP-Transfer scored 48.3 with its signature pruning step effectively disabled. Reach for it when you need a number for an endpoint you cannot afford to attack interactively. Same paper as TAP.

GPTFuzz — GPTFUZZER: Red Teaming LLMs with Auto-Generated Jailbreak Prompts

local vLLM target + local judge (open source only)class GPTFuzzmax 1,000 queries / 100 iterationsno Table 6 column

Start with the gap, because it is the most useful fact here. GPTFuzz has an implementation in baselines/gptfuzz/, a config file and an active row in run_pipeline.yaml — and it is not one of Table 6's sixteen columns, nor one of the sixteen text-only methods the paper lists in §6. There is no published HarmBench ASR for it. The only numbers HarmBench attaches to the name belong to GPTFuzz the classifier: 75.42% human agreement in Table 3 and 65.8 average on the prequalification sets in Table 4, including 35.2 on completions harmful for the wrong behavior. A GPTFuzz attack ASR quoted as a HarmBench result did not come from this paper.

The mechanism is mutation-based fuzzing with a bandit over seeds. The seed file is a CSV of 77 in-the-wild jailbreak templates — id and text columns, 260 to 5,498 characters, median around 1,756 — each with a slot the behavior is spliced into. Per iteration the fuzzer picks a seed by Monte-Carlo tree search over past reward, applies one of five gpt-3.5-turbo mutation operators at temperature 0 (crossover, expand, generate-similar, rephrase, shorten), queries the target for max_new_tokens: 512 and scores the reply. energy: 1, max_jailbreak: 1, max_query: 1000 and max_iteration: 100 cap it.

Two config-versus-code discrepancies are worth having in hand. seed_selection_strategy: round_robin is stored on the object and never read — the driver hardcodes the MCTS policy. And the internal judge is hubert233/GPTFuzz, the same RoBERTa that Table 4 scores at 35.2 on wrong-behavior completions, so the fuzzer can stop early on a completion that is harmful but not this behavior; the HarmBench classifier then grades that as a miss. Reach for it when you have weights and want cheap breadth from a template corpus. Yu et al., 2023, arXiv:2309.10253 (verified).

PAP-top5 — How Johnny Can Persuade LLMs to Jailbreak Them

no target access at allclass PAP, experiment top_55 generations, 0 target queriesavg ASR 16.6

PAP starts from a taxonomy of forty persuasion techniques drawn from the social-science literature, each stored as a name, a definition and a worked example. top_k_persuasion_taxonomy: 5 takes the first five in the ordering the PAP paper's Figure 7 established: Logical Appeal, Authority Endorsement, Misrepresentation, Evidence-based Persuasion, Expert Endorsement. Mixtral 8x7B rewrites the request once per technique, and for contextual behaviors the ContextString is prepended to each result.

That is the whole method: no judge in the loop, no target query, no iteration — five generations and done. PAP is here because a language model writes its prompts, not because it searches; structurally it is closer to ZeroShot than to PAIR. The denominator consequence matters: PAP emits five test cases per behavior and all five are graded, while every other method here emits one curated best. 16.6 is the mean success of five unguided rewrites, not of one optimised prompt. HarmBench also ran the in-context variant rather than PAP's fine-tuned paraphraser, which was not public.

The column shape is instructive: 13.9 standard, 31.3 contextual — giving the rewriter a technical passage to be persuasive about more than doubles it — 7.4 copyright. On permissive models PAP is worse than simply asking (32.9 against DirectRequest's 65.8 on Zephyr 7B): rhetoric gives an already-compliant model something to refuse. On the hardest it flips — 2.7 / 3.3 / 4.1 across the three Llama 2 Chat sizes against DirectRequest's 0.8 / 2.8 / 2.8. So 16.6 understates what this represents: five generations, zero target queries, fluent natural language with no adversarial suffix for a perplexity filter to catch, where GCG's 54.3 needs a 500-step gradient search and leaves an artefact any input filter can see coming. PAP-avg (the full_40 experiment) sits commented out of run_pipeline.yaml as too expensive on closed models, so the full taxonomy has no number. Zeng et al., 2024, arXiv:2401.06373 (verified).

What the family's numbers say

The headline is a cost result, not a capability result. GCG tops the table at 54.3 on a 500-step search with 512 gradient-scored candidates per step, per behavior. AutoDAN reaches 52.7 on 100 generations of sentence-shuffling and ~58 calls to a 7B paraphraser; TAP reaches 45.2 on 94 chat turns; PAP reaches 16.6 on five generations. The gradient family's entire advantage over the best gradient-free method is 1.6 points — inside the ~7% label noise implied by the classifier's 93.19% human agreement, so GCG and AutoDAN are not distinguishable on this evidence. And this family is the closed-model evaluation: down the GPT, Claude and Gemini rows of Table 6 the only non-dash entries come from here, from GCG-Transfer (22.0 and 22.3 on the two GPT-4 endpoints, against TAP-Transfer's 54.8 and 58.5) and from the template baselines. Without PAIR, TAP and TAP-Transfer there would be almost nothing to report about frontier closed models — though the Claude column keeps that honest, with every method here between 0.8 and 10.0 and the transfer variant doing worst.

Two things to carry away. Each method steers on a proxy — a Mixtral rating capped at five output tokens, a RoBERTa classifier that cannot tell which behavior a completion serves, a list of refusal substrings — while the real grader is a better model applied afterwards to a 512-token completion that PAIR and TAP never saw more than 150 tokens of. These ASRs measure how often a search steered by a weak, partial signal lands somewhere a strong grader accepts, and that gap closes in the attacker's favour as the proxy improves. Then read all six against the floor: DirectRequest averages 25.3 by sending the behavior unchanged and Human Jailbreaks 27.3, so PAP-top5's 16.6 is below both, while PAIR's 40.7 is about 15 points of genuine lift over asking and TAP's 45.2 is 20. Those deltas, not the raw ASRs, are what a new method has to beat. The IDs behind these columns are in the behavior index, the splits behind each average in the results read.
